**How Much Tax Does the UK Actually Pay? 2026 Data Study**

Most people know their income tax and NI deductions from their payslip, but the full picture of UK taxation is much broader. This data study quantifies every major tax a typical UK employee pays across salary levels from £20,000 to £200,000.

## Tax Layers Explained

UK workers pay taxes in several layers:

1. **Income Tax** — 20% / 40% / 45% on earnings above £12,570
2. **National Insurance** — 8% / 2% on earnings above £12,570
3. **Pension contributions** — minimum 5% of qualifying earnings (auto-enrolment)
4. **VAT** — 20% on most goods and services
5. **Council Tax** — varies by band and local authority
6. **Fuel duty, alcohol duty, stamp duty** — additional consumption taxes

## Adding VAT: The Hidden Tax

VAT (Value Added Tax) is charged at **20%** on most goods and services. While you don't see it deducted from your payslip, it's a significant tax on your spending.

## The £100,000 Tax Trap in Detail

Between £100,000 and £125,140, the loss of the Personal Allowance creates a **60% marginal rate** — you keep only 40p of every £1 earned. This is the highest effective marginal rate outside the additional rate band.

| Earnings Increment | Marginal Income Tax | Marginal NI | **Marginal Rate** |
|--------------------|--------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| £12,571 → £50,270 | 20% | 8% | **28%** |
| £50,271 → £100,000 | 40% | 2% | **42%** |
| £100,001 → £125,140 | 40% + 20% (PA loss) | 2% | **62%** |
| Above £125,140 | 45% | 2% | **47%** |
