**Emergency Tax Codes UK Explained**

If your payslip shows a tax code ending in **W1** (week 1), **M1** (month 1), or **X**, you're on an emergency tax code. This means you could be paying too much tax. This guide explains why and how to fix it.

## What Is an Emergency Tax Code?

An emergency tax code is used when HMRC doesn't have complete information about your earnings. The most common emergency code for 2025/26 is **1257L W1** or **1257L M1**:

- **W1** = Week 1 (for weekly-paid employees)
- **M1** = Month 1 (for monthly-paid employees)
- **X** = used in some payroll systems instead of W1/M1

These suffixes mean your tax is calculated on a **non-cumulative** basis — each pay period is taxed independently without reference to what you've earned earlier in the tax year. This often results in **overpaying tax** because your Personal Allowance isn't properly applied.

Read our [how to read a payslip guide](/how-to-read-payslip-uk/) to understand where your tax code appears.

## Why Are You on an Emergency Tax Code?

Common reasons:

| Situation | What Happens |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Starting a new job** | Employer doesn't have your P45 yet |
| **No P45 provided** | HMRC has no record of your previous earnings |
| **Multiple jobs** | HMRC can't determine your primary employment |
| **Tax code change** | HMRC is updating your code mid-year |
| **New to the UK** | No UK tax history on file |
| **Returning to work** | After a gap, HMRC needs updated information |

## How Emergency Tax Works (Example)

**Normal (cumulative) tax code 1257L:**
- You earn £2,500/month
- Monthly Personal Allowance: £1,047.50 (£12,570 / 12)
- Taxable: £1,452.50 at 20% = **£290.50 tax/month**

**Emergency code 1257L M1 (same salary):**
- Same calculation per month
- BUT if you started in Month 7 (October), you'd have been taxed as if earning £2,500 from Month 1
- You'd owe **£2,034** in tax in Month 7 instead of £290 — because prior months' untaxed allowance is ignored
- This overpayment is recovered when your correct code arrives

**Result:** You could temporarily overpay hundreds of pounds until HMRC corrects your code.

## How to Get Off an Emergency Tax Code

### Method 1: Wait (Automatic)
In most cases, HMRC will automatically update your code once they receive your employment details (usually within 4-8 weeks). Your employer will then apply the correct code and any overpaid tax is refunded via your payslip.

### Method 2: Provide Your P45
Give your P45 from your previous employer to your new employer's payroll department. They'll use it to set the correct tax code immediately.

### Method 3: Update HMRC Online
1. Sign in to your [personal tax account](https://www.gov.uk/personal-tax-account) on GOV.UK
2. Go to "Check your Income Tax" → "Tell HMRC about a change"
3. Update your employment details
4. HMRC will issue a new tax code to your employer

### Method 4: Call HMRC
If the online method doesn't work, call HMRC on **0300 200 3300**. Have your National Insurance number ready.

Source: [GOV.UK — Check your Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/check-income-tax-current-year).

## Will You Get a Tax Refund?

If you've overpaid tax on an emergency code, you'll usually get it back automatically:
- **During the tax year:** Your employer refunds the overpayment when your correct code is applied
- **After the tax year ends:** HMRC sends a calculation (P800) and arranges a refund

You can also claim a refund yourself at [GOV.UK — Claim a tax refund](https://www.gov.uk/claim-tax-refund).
