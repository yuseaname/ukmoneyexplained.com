**Council Tax Reduction UK: How to Get Help with Your Bill**

If you're struggling to pay your Council Tax, you may be entitled to a reduction. Council Tax Reduction (CTR) — sometimes called Council Tax Support (CTS) — can significantly lower or even eliminate your bill. This guide explains who qualifies and how to apply.

## Who Qualifies for Council Tax Reduction?

You may be eligible if you:

- Are on a **low income** (no fixed threshold — each council decides)
- Claim **Universal Credit**, Jobseeker's Allowance, or Pension Credit
- Are a **student** (students are usually exempt)
- Live alone (you're entitled to a **25% Single Person Discount** regardless of income)
- Are a **carer** for someone in the household
- Have a **severe mental impairment** (SMI) or live with someone who does

Each council in England and Wales sets its own CTR scheme, so eligibility and reduction amounts vary by area. In Scotland, the scheme is more generous — many low-income households pay **zero Council Tax**.

## How Much Can You Save?

The reduction depends on your circumstances, income, savings, and household:

| Situation | Typical Reduction |
|-----------|------------------|
| On Universal Credit, low income | 50–100% (varies by council) |
| Single person (25% discount) | 25% (automatic if registered) |
| Pensioner on Pension Credit | Up to 100% in many areas |
| Disabled person (band reduction) | Bill reduced to the band below |
| Student household | 100% exemption |
| Second adult rebate (if another adult can't pay) | Up to 25% |

Source: [GOV.UK — Council Tax exemptions and discounts](https://www.gov.uk/council-tax/exemptions-and-discounts).

## How to Apply

1. **Find your council** at [GOV.UK — Find your local council](https://www.gov.uk/find-local-council)
2. **Visit their website** and search for "Council Tax Reduction" or "Council Tax Support"
3. **Complete the application** — you'll need:
   - Your Council Tax account number (on your bill)
   - National Insurance number
   - Proof of income (payslips, benefit letters)
   - Proof of savings (bank statements)
   - Details of everyone in your household
4. **Apply as soon as possible** — reductions are usually backdated to the date you applied, not earlier

## Council Tax Reduction and Universal Credit

If you claim Universal Credit, you do **not** automatically get Council Tax Reduction. You must apply separately through your local council. However, being on Universal Credit makes you eligible to apply — your council will use your UC assessment to determine the reduction.

Read our [Universal Credit explained guide](/universal-credit-explained-uk/) for more on how UC works.

## Other Ways to Reduce Your Council Tax

- **Challenge your band:** If you think your property is in the wrong band, you can challenge it. Read our [Council Tax bands guide](/council-tax-bands-explained-2026/).
- **Disability band reduction:** If someone in your home has a disability and needs extra space (e.g., a ground-floor bedroom or extra bathroom), your bill may be reduced to the band below. Source: [GOV.UK — Disabled band reduction](https://www.gov.uk/apply-for-council-tax-disability-band-reduction).
- **Backdating:** If you were entitled to CTR but didn't claim, some councils will backdate your reduction — always ask.

## What If You Can't Pay?

If you're behind on Council Tax:
1. **Contact your council immediately** — they have a duty to help
2. **Apply for CTR** as above
3. **Ask for a payment plan** — councils must consider reasonable offers
4. **Check for hardship funds** — many councils have discretionary funds
5. **Seek free debt advice** from [StepChange](https://www.stepchange.org/) or [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk)

Council Tax arrears are a **priority debt** — councils can take enforcement action including bailiffs and imprisonment (in extreme cases). Don't ignore the problem.
